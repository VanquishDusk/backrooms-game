# The Backrooms

A replayable 3D horror game set in the Backrooms, playable in the browser on desktop and mobile.

Playable now: **Level 0 — "The Lobby" / "The Yellow Hell"**, its sub-level **Level 0.2 — "The Remodel"**, **Level 1 — "The Habitable Zone"**, **Level 1.3**, **Level 1.5 — "Inverted"**, **Level 2 — "Abandoned Utility Halls"**, **Level 3 — "Electrical Station"**, **Level 4 — "Abandoned Office"**, **Level 5 — "Terror Hotel"**, **Level 6 — "Lights Out"**, **Level 7 — "Thalassophobia"**, **Level 14 — "Paradise"**, **Level 15 — "Futuristic Halls"**, **Level 63 — "Tranquility"**, **Level 94 — "Motion"** and **Level 601 — "The End"**. More levels will follow.

## Play

You can serve the folder with any static file server. There's no build step.

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000
```

It also works on GitHub Pages: enable Pages for the branch and open the site URL.

## Level 0

You've noclipped out of reality into endless mono-yellow halls, damp carpet and buzzing fluorescent lights.

- **Find the flickering wall** and throw yourself through it. It's in a different place every run. Listen for its static.
- **Sanity** drains in the hum. Darkness, the red rooms and things you shouldn't see drain it faster.
- **Almond Water** steadies your mind.
- **Buzzing spikes:** the lights get painfully loud. Cover your ears and keep moving until you're out of it.
- **The maze shifts** where you aren't looking.
- **Whispers, distant voices, scratching behind the walls, footsteps that aren't yours.**
- **A dark figure** watches from the end of the corridors. If your sanity slips far enough, it stops watching.
- **The Pitfalls:** a room full of holes in the floor. Watch your step.
- **Dark zones** where the lights died (use your flashlight), and a **red room**.
- **Exit:** throw yourself through the flickering wall to reach Level 1.

Some layouts have a pocket where the buzzing quiets, the carpet dries out and the rooms look half-built. If you find the **door** there, it leads to Level 0.2.

## Level 0.2 — The Remodel (Survival Difficulty 3)

Level 0 as remodelled by the Backrooms Remodeling Co. It has dry red carpet, fresh white paint, working outlets and the odd bathroom. A few seconds after you arrive, it starts falling apart.

- A **chain reaction** spreads out from where you came in. Ceiling tiles fall, walls topple and the floor caves in to show the unfinished house underneath. Debris hurts you.
- **Dust** chokes you. Cover your mouth (same button/key as covering your ears) and get out of it.
- Find the **door back to Level 0** before the collapse reaches it. Level 0's hum leaks through it, and there's light under the door. If the collapse gets there first, there's no way back.
- **Outlets** charge your flashlight.
- Going back puts you in a fresh Level 0. Your sanity, health, battery and Almond Water carry over.

## Level 1 — The Habitable Zone (Survival Difficulty 1)

An endless, abandoned concrete complex: half underground car park, half empty warehouse.

- **Halls:** huge pillared spaces with damp floors, exposed pipes and long sightlines. This is where you explore, and where things happen.
- **Corridors:** concrete hallways with offices and infirmaries. They're safer, and red emergency lighting stays on in a blackout.
- **Storage:** shelving and crates.
- **Sectors:**
  - **Aquila:** the garage where you arrive, with an explorers' cache and a note.
  - **Gild:** warehouse, full of crates.
  - **Gothic:** vaulted round columns and a medical station.
  - **Ouroboros:** construction that never ends, with workers who never look at you.
  - **Garden:** overgrown and harmful to stay in, but with better loot.
  - **Fabled:** old wood panelling and neon.
- **Blackouts:** the lights die and something hunts the halls. It won't follow you into the corridors.
- **Impossible geometry:** halls stretch away from you, and corridor walls move when you aren't looking.
- **Crates** hold Almond Water, batteries, first aid kits, junk, or something worse. Some vanish when you look away, and new ones turn up.
- **Exit:** follow an extended hallway where pipes and dials gradually appear. Pipes on the ceiling get denser as you get closer, and you can hear steam ahead. Toward the end it gets more industrial: false walls step in at 45 degrees, the ceiling drops, and fat lagged pipes, gauges and caged bulbs crowd in until you walk into Level 2.

Near the explorers' cache, a spotless **white wall** leads to Level 1.3.

## Level 1.3 — The Ward (Survival Difficulty 0… or a Dead Zone)

One long, blinding-white tiled hallway with branching paths to 16 large rooms: Detox Zones (marked in green tape), medical supply rooms, and The Ward. Each visit rolls which Level 1.3 you get:

- **The sanctuary:** Detox Zones heal you quickly (it burns), M.E.G. doctors staff The Ward, the supply rooms are stocked, and heart monitors beep.
- **The Dead Zone:** the Decay has got in.
  - Black voids eat into the floors, walls and ceilings, and they keep spreading. Step into one and you're gone.
  - Dried blood, covered bodies, a flatlined monitor.
  - The Detox Zones heal you for a moment, then tear you apart.
  - Recovered documents tell what happened.
  - One end of the hallway is already being swallowed.
- **Exit:** noclip into either end of the main hallway to return to Level 1.

## Level 1.5 — Inverted (Survival Difficulty unknown)

Reached through a **fake window** set into a concrete corridor wall in Level 1. Through the glass you can see a hallway in negative.

- **The look:** a house hallway shown as a monochrome photo negative.
  - Ceiling fixtures radiate darkness, and the dark ends of the hallways glow white.
  - Picture frames and door trim shine, and the floor at your feet is black.
  - Walls lean and sway, and the camera sits slightly tilted.
- **Upside-down architecture:** chairs and tables hang from the ceiling, fixtures are set into the floor, some doors are hung upside-down with the knob near the top, some pictures are upside-down, and there's backwards writing on the walls. Every so often the whole view turns over, and the controls adjust so they still feel right.
- **Silence:** there's no hum. Denizens drift through the halls whispering, and their whispers get **quieter as you get closer**. If one passes through you, you lose sanity and end up somewhere else.
- **She** only moves while you aren't looking at her.
- **Logs** from the explorer who got trapped here are scattered on the floor, garbled.
- **Exit:** the only hum here is Level 0's, leaking through the one place reality is thin. Follow it to a glitching dead end where the negative keeps failing, and noclip through. You usually land in Level 1, sometimes Level 0.

## Level 2 — Abandoned Utility Halls (Survival Difficulty 2)

Reached by following Level 1's pipe hallway until it narrows into a maintenance tunnel.

- **The tunnels:** a labyrinth of narrow (about 2 m) concrete maintenance tunnels with long straight runs, branches, loops and dead ends.
  - Turns are cut at **45 degrees**, so the tunnels bend instead of meeting at square corners.
  - Walls are chalky, peeling whitewash over board-formed concrete, with rust runs and damp. Some sections are old dark brick.
- **Pipes everywhere:**
  - Every wall carries a bundle that runs continuously along the tunnel, follows each bend and turns corners. It only dives into the wall or rises into the ceiling where the wall ends.
  - Bundles mix fat cream-lagged pipes, rusty iron, red-painted pipe, galvanised pipe, conduit and cable, all on strut-channel brackets.
  - Some walls have a raised concrete ledge instead.
  - Ceilings carry pipe runs, big spiral ducts or cable trays on threaded-rod hangers. There are valves, flanges, gauges and steam leaks.
- **Plant rooms:** small rooms with diamond-plate floors, boilers, pumps, tanks, generators and switchgear. You can hear them whirring from the tunnels.
- **Clutter:** drums, gas bottles, cable spools, cardboard boxes, toolboxes, rubble, paper and broken fluorescent tubes. Wall signs say DANGER, HIGH VOLTAGE or KEEP CLEAR, or give section codes.
- **Light and dark:**
  - Lights are harsh bare fluorescent tubes or orange caged bulbs. Many are dead, some flicker, and there are long dark gaps between them.
  - Some sections are almost entirely dark.
  - Every so often a whole section loses power at once and stays dark for a while.
  - Darkness wears on your sanity, so use the flashlight.
- **Sound:** fluorescent buzz, motors and pumps, water hammer knocking down the pipes, distant clanks, drips and steam.
- **Entities:** Hounds, Smilers (only in the dark), Skin-Stealers, Deathmoths (around the lights, and your flashlight) and Clumps (waiting in dead ends).
- **Doors:** steel maintenance doors sit at the back of dead ends and in plant-room walls. Almost all are locked. A rare few are unlocked, and each looks and sounds different:
  - **Faded green with a chalk "1"**, under a lone bulb, with water dripping nearby. It leads back to **Level 1**.
  - **Rust red with hazard tape and a lightning bolt**, with a pulsing red lamp and arcing electricity. It leads on to **Level 3**.
  - **Sometimes, a wooden office door with a brass "4"**, with warm light spilling under it and a scrap of carpet in front. It leads to **Level 4**.
- **Supply crates** sit in some dead ends: Almond Water, batteries, first aid, junk, or a burst of steam.

## Level 3 — Electrical Station (Survival Difficulty 4)

Reached through Level 2's red hazard-taped door. You arrive in front of it, and walking back into it returns you to Level 2.

- **The station:** a dark maze of brick service corridors.
  - Walls are unevenly coloured red, dark or tan brick, or glazed brown tile.
  - Floors are dusty concrete or worn grey tile, under a peeling ceiling and dim buzzing tubes.
  - Wiring, conduit and junction boxes line the walls, along with pipes of **black fluid** you can see moving through brass-collared sight glasses. It leaks into black puddles.
- **Low passages:** some stretches are so low you automatically **crouch** or **crawl** (slower, no sprinting). Skin-Stealers can't follow you into the crawlspaces.
- **Bars:**
  - Indestructible bars block many corridors, some with locked gates and OUT OF BOUNDS signs.
  - Whole **galleries** are sealed behind bars: brightly lit, with damaged angel statues, paintings, a chair facing the wall and a television showing static.
  - Sometimes a **shadow** stands in a gallery and watches you.
- **Generator rooms:**
  - Green steel switchgear with copper busbars, insulators, fuse banks and cables, plus a generator set and control cabinets.
  - Switchgear and loose wires **spark**, and getting too close hurts.
  - When a **generator fails** you hear it wind down, and a whole section goes dark.
- **Boiler rooms:** riveted brass boiler drums, a pear-shaped vessel, pumps, brass risers with handwheels, and a glowing furnace. The heat makes the air shimmer.
- **Assembly hall:** a huge tall hall with running conveyors, stamping presses, a gantry crane and dome lamps. The output bins **keep producing supplies** (Almond Water, batteries, first aid).
- **Sanctums:** tall, dark, cathedral-like rooms.
  - Inside are marble floors, columns, pews, candles, sacred paintings and a huge **angel statue**.
  - **Entities won't enter.** Your sanity recovers inside, and you can't be hurt there.
- **Base Gamma:** an empty M.E.G. outpost.
  - Bunks, a radio tuned to nothing, supply crates and a note.
  - Entities stay out of here too.
  - From the base, **painted arrows on the floor** lead to the lift.
- **Hazards:**
  - Lights sometimes **shatter** near you.
  - **Purple discoloration** tints your vision and drains your sanity.
  - The heat makes your stamina recover more slowly.
- **Entities:** Hounds and Skin-Stealers.
- **Exit:** an old **freight lift**, a wood-panelled car with a scissor gate, a LIFT sign and a floor dial. Its motor hums and its bell rings now and then. Step inside to go down to **Level 4**. When there's a second lift, it goes down to **Level 5** instead (and now and then the only one does).

## Level 4 — Abandoned Office (Survival Difficulty 1)

Reached by Level 3's freight lift or Level 2's wooden office door. It's the safe one.

- **The office:** an empty office floor that goes on and on.
  - Huge open-plan floors with square columns, dark blue-grey carpet and a white tile ceiling full of vents and square light panels, many of them off.
  - The perimeter is bright with washed-out daylight through windows hung with faded peach curtains, radiators beneath and pilasters between. The middle of each floor stays gloomy.
  - Long, straight carpeted corridors.
  - Off-white walls with grey baseboards and office doors standing open.
  - Rooms with almost nothing in them: a desk and chair here, a filing cabinet there, a chair facing the wall.
  - Some open-plan floors keep a few cubicles. There are conference rooms, break rooms with a counter, a microwave and a table, and storage rooms full of stacked chairs.
- **Windows:** most just show white daylight with nothing beyond it, and some are blacked out with taped plastic sheeting. A few show a hazy, too-bright city. **Don't stand and look out of them:** you'll get a warning to look away, and then something looks back.
- **Almond Water is everywhere:**
  - Vending machines and water coolers fill your bottles. They run out eventually.
  - Drinking fountains restore sanity straight away.
- **Communities:** a **M.E.G. outpost** (a banner, supplies, bunks and a note) and a **wanderers' camp** (sleeping bags, a lantern and a note).
- **Entities:** very few. A Hound somewhere, sometimes a Duller, and maybe a Smiler in one of the dark rooms. Your sanity barely drains here.
- **Sound:** fluorescent hum, the air handling, a phone ringing somewhere, a distant elevator bell.
- **Exits** are in the closed service cores:
  - Stairwells down to **Level 5**.
  - Elevators to **Level 5**, and sometimes one back to **Level 3**. The display over the door shows which.
  - A dark doorway under a dead EXIT sign to **Level 6**.
  - Often, a door with a glowing neon **EXIT** sign and white daylight behind it, as if it led out of the Backrooms. It leads to **Level 601**.
  - Rarely, a **basement** stairway to **Level 71**.
  - **Exits move:** once you've seen one, lose sight of it and it may be gone when you come back, standing at another core instead.
- Levels 6 and 71 are coming soon.

## Level 5 — Terror Hotel (Survival Difficulty 2)

Reached by Level 4's stairwells and elevators, or one of Level 3's freight lifts. An endless 1920s hotel, and its boiler room.

- **The Main Hall:**
  - Long corridors of numbered mahogany doors, most of them locked. Burgundy damask above dark wainscoting, a red carpet with a gold lattice, brass lantern sconces, side tables, paintings, abandoned room service carts.
  - Open rooms behind some doors: a bed and nightstands, a wardrobe, a writing desk and mirror, a lamp. Suites have a sofa.
  - **Lobbies and lounges** where the corridors meet: crimson faux-marble columns with gilded rope edges and capitals carrying dark girders painted with a frieze, a beamed ochre ceiling, wrought-iron candle chandeliers, lanterns on the columns, a medallion rug on sage carpet, striped armchairs and sofas round pedestal tables with white orchids, potted palms.
  - **The grand lobby** has a reception desk with key cubbies and a desk bell, a brass luggage cart, a grandfather clock, arched front doors glowing with an afternoon that never ends (they're locked), and the **grand stair**.
  - **The Homely Hotel:** a dining room with a buffet. Eat to heal, and take meat with you. Samantha the cat lives here and trades readings for meat.
  - **M.E.G. Housekeeping:** shelves of linen, a cot, crates of supplies and a note.
  - Letters from **The Originals** have been slipped under a few doors.
- **The Beverly Room (the Eternal Ballroom):** parquet, gilt pilasters, tall mirrors and velvet drapes, a coffered ceiling, crystal chandeliers, round tables set for dinner, a drinks table, a mahjong game left mid-hand, and a bandstand with a piano, a double bass and drums. Nobody is playing, but the music never stops: smooth jazz, loud by the bandstand and muffled through the walls everywhere else.
- **The Boiler Room** (through STAFF ONLY doors and the ballroom's back door):
  - Narrow brick passages lined with bundles of white lagged pipe, rusty and yellow gas lines, valves and caged bulbs.
  - **Boiler halls** with big grey-blue fire-tube boilers (burner, fan, inspection ports, rusty risers), green and yellow tanks, barrels, workbenches and debris.
  - **Catwalk galleries:** a narrow green catwalk with black railings over a pit full of machinery and lagged pipe.
  - Tank rooms and pump rooms.
  - **Almond Water** runs in the pipes: fill bottles at the taps.
  - The deeper you go, the hotter it gets. In the deepest halls the heat hurts you, slowly.
- **Entities:** Deathmoths everywhere (circling the chandeliers and sconces), Hounds, Woodlins, Skin-Stealers, a Watcher in the ballroom, Nguithr'xurh on the corridor ceilings, Deathrats, and in the boiler room a Growler and a Wrangler. Samantha in the Homely Hotel.
- **Exits:**
  - The **grand stair** in the grand lobby goes back up to **Level 4**. You arrive at its foot.
  - **Elevators:** bronze art deco doors with a floor dial above. Floors 1 to 12 take you to another elevator elsewhere in the hotel. One goes down to **Level 3**. One goes to **13**: don't. Some dials twitch towards 13 now and then.
  - The **maintenance lift** in the boiler room. Not recommended.
  - A **dark wooden door** with no number among the guest rooms: **Level 9**.
  - Rarely, a **plain metal door** among the wooden ones, with a porthole full of sky and cold daylight leaking under it: **Level 63**.
  - Stairs by the **furnace**, in the deepest boiler hall, go down to **Level 6**.
- Level 9 is coming soon.

## Level 6 — Lights Out (Survival Difficulty pending)

Reached by the stairs by the furnace (or the maintenance lift) in Level 5's boiler room, or through the dark doorway in Level 4.

- **The halls:** endless narrow hallways of smooth, cold concrete, one person wide, a maze of long straights, junctions and dead ends.
- **Complete darkness:** your flashlight clicks and nothing comes out, and flares won't catch. Your eyes adjust just enough to make out a wall at arm's length; past that, nothing.
- **Silence, then not:** no hum, no machines. The longer you're here the more you hear: whispering, breathing right behind you, something scuttling, footsteps that aren't yours. Your sanity drains the whole time, faster if you stand still. Almond Water helps.
- **Possibly an entity:** nothing confirmed. But when your mind starts to go, something may come up the hall behind you, breathing. Keep moving and it loses you; let it reach you and it hurts.
- **Wrong turns:** the door back to Level 5 is right where you arrive, and slams shut once you go far enough in. Take a branch that leads away from the way out and the hall may grind shut behind you once you're out of sight. Usually another way opens somewhere else; sometimes the way to Level 7 is simply gone.
- **Exits:**
  - A **stairwell** deep inside goes to **Level 7**. Listen for faint waves: they get louder, and pull to one side, along the way that actually leads there.
  - Rarely, a huge **frost-covered metal door**, so cold you feel it before you find it: **Level 129**.
  - **Wires** strung across the floor at ankle height. Walk carefully and you'll usually step over; run and you'll trip and fall right through into **Level 6.1**.
- Levels 129 and 6.1 are coming soon.

## Level 7 — Thalassophobia (Survival Difficulty 4)

Reached by the stairwell deep in Level 6.

- **The entrance room:** a flooded living room high up inside a huge concrete pillar: a bookcase, a coffee table, an armchair, chests of drawers stacked against the wall with pot plants on top, a buzzing fluorescent lamp, and a few centimetres of water over the carpet. The stairs behind you go back up to **Level 6**.
- **The doorway:** gravity is different in this room. Get close to the open door and "down" starts to mean "out": it pulls you in, the room tilts, and you fall fourteen metres into the sea.
- **The ocean:** grey water stretching away forever under a concrete ceiling, in a dim light with no source. Giant concrete pillars rise out of the sea into the ceiling. A mountain looms in the distance.
- **Swimming:** you swim where you look; hold **C** / **Space** (**UP** on touch) to rise. Watch **BREATH** underwater and **WARMTH**: the water is freezing and the cold kills you slowly. Get out of the water to warm up. Being out on the open sea wears on your sanity too.
- **Depth zones:** the **Daylight Zone** (bright, sandy, empty), the **Twilight Zone** (dark, bones and rusted metal), the **Midnight Zone** (pitch black and full of skeletons; your flashlight works here) and the **Abyss**.
- **The boat:** a small open boat floats near where you land, with a lantern at the bow and a crate of harpoons. Swim up to it to climb in. Steer with WASD / the stick (Shift for full throttle). **C** / **Space** (**JUMP**) goes over the side. It has a **hull**: hit things, get shot or get rammed and it breaks apart.
- **The Thing on Level 7:** a sea serpent some seventy metres long, with milky eyes, a pale belly, spines down its back and spots that glow when it's angry. It stays down in the deep until you go out into open water. Then:
  - In the boat, it circles you just under the surface, then either **charges** with its head out of the water or **rears up** over the boat, roars and crashes down where you were. Throw harpoons (**click** / **R** / **THROW**) at its head when it surfaces; body hits do half as much. Two hits and it breaks off its attack. Drive away when it rears up. Enough hits and it thrashes, screams and sinks into the deep for good. The harpoons stay stuck in it.
  - In the water, it hunts you, and you can't fight back.
- **Fort Surrender:** a rocky island with shacks, a log palisade, a watchtower, lanterns and a white flag. They shout at you to turn back, and shoot at anyone who comes close.
- **Exits:**
  - The stairs back up to **Level 6**.
  - An **underwater cave** at the foot of the mountain, down in the Midnight Zone: **Level 8**.
  - A **wooden door** standing on an underwater stone platform among stone columns and giant rusted pipes that rise into the ceiling. Warm light leaks out under it: **Level 9**.
  - Dive too deep into the **Abyss** and you black out: **Level 83**.
  - Keep going **west** across the sea, far enough, and you reach **Level 880**.
- Levels 8, 83 and 880 are coming soon.

## Level 14 — Paradise (Survival Difficulty: Paradise)

Isn't it pretty? You should visit sometime.

- **Ways in:** look out of one of Level 4's windows for too long and, sometimes, what's out there isn't a thing but a place, and you step through. Or fall off the edge of Level 63: sometimes you land softly on red leaves instead of waking up somewhere you know.
- **The forest:** endless tall bare trunks reaching up at an enormous moon and a sky full of stars and a band of milky way. Crimson leaves underfoot, short red grass wet with dew, leaves drifting down, motes of light, violet and blue fog banks drifting between the trees.
- **The waterfalls:** every so often a great cliff of wet rock, three falls crashing into a black pool that reflects the moon, mist rising off it.
- **The cacophony:** the forest whispers to you, kindly ("You'll be happy here. Everyone else is."). Underneath, a wordless choir, slightly out of tune, which gets louder and more out of tune as your mind goes. Your **sanity drains everywhere**, faster the lower it is. Covering your ears slows it. The **roar of a waterfall drowns the choir out**: close to the falling water your sanity comes back, and you can drink from the pool.
- **Your mind leaking:** below half sanity your feet drift towards the guests, and your view sways. Very low, you start to float.
- **The guests:** a clearing where people lie peacefully in the soft soil in a ring, bones glistening in the moonlight, pale flowers growing out of them. It pulls you in and slows you down. Stand still in the middle and you lie down with them.
- **The children:** pale, hollow-eyed figures standing in rows, heads thrown back, howling at the moon. Don't get close, don't stare at them, and don't shine a light at them. When the howling stops, every head turns, and the nearest come for you in jerky bursts. They won't go near the waterfalls. Weapons stun them.
- **Ways out (you won't have to worry about that):**
  - The **cave behind the biggest waterfall**: cold and quiet, your thoughts come back, and the rock turns to concrete. **Level 1**.
  - Swim out to the **deep middle of the pool** and go under: **Level 7**.
  - A **white steel door** standing on its own among the trees, cold light round its edges, machines humming behind it: **Level 15**.

## Level 15 — Futuristic Halls (Survival Difficulty 0)

Reached through the white door in Level 14.

- **The halls:** an endless grid of white barrel-vaulted halls with white steel ribs, two big steel ducts along the crown, lamps throwing light up into the vault, a polished terrazzo floor, glowing panels down one wall and mint-green tiles down the other, and a band of strange symbols along every wall. Some halls are completely dark.
- **Behind the reinforced doors** (yellow frames, half-open steel doors, a symbol over each): a machine still making steel rods on a conveyor, a machine so big the room was built around it, wrecked and burnt machinery (with a dead Hound), laboratories, dormitories with bunks and lockers, kitchens, control rooms with walls of screens, storage. People in lab coats lie where they fell.
- **Safe and silent:** no entities, but there's **no Almond Water anywhere**, and the dark halls wear on you.
- **Enric:** the only other person here, in his camp in a big control room next to where you arrive. He's been here two years. Talk to him (walk up): he'll give you a bottle of Almond Water and tell you about the computers.
- **The I.L.N.:** the computers still work. Stand at one to read it: the files are in a script nobody can read. Three of them hold pieces Enric has half translated. Read all three and they fit together into a map, and you're told roughly which way and how far to go. You'll hear a draught of wind from that direction.
- **The way out:** a door with **no symbol** over it, and behind it a stairway up into daylight. Climb it: **you made it**. (This ends your run, as an escape.)


## Level 63 — Tranquility (Survival Difficulty 0)

Reached through the rare metal doors in Level 5.

- **High in the sky:** weathered wooden platforms on stilts, joined by sagging rope bridges and plank bridges with railings, ladders up to a second tier of smaller decks, and **monkey bars** across some gaps. Below, nothing: an endless blue void with clouds drifting beneath you.
- **The vines:** huge green walls of vine rise out of the void in the distance, and keep unravelling and knitting themselves into new patterns.
- **The sky:** deep blue with cumulus clouds that form, drift through and thin away. The sun crosses the sky, sets and rises again, but it never gets dark.
- **Meditation Points:** round platforms with a single bench at the end of a bridge. It's quieter there. Stand still by the bench to sit down: your sanity and health come back quickly and your thoughts settle. Move to stand up.
- **Climbing:** walk into a ladder to climb it. On monkey bars you hang on for as long as your stamina lasts; let it run out and you fall.
- **Falling:** step off an edge with no railing, or lose your grip, and you fall into the void. It isn't fatal: you lose consciousness and wake up somewhere safer (Level 4 or Level 1). Usually.
- **Entities:** none. Only wind, creaking rope and the distant rustle of the vines.

## Level 601 — The End (Survival Difficulty unknown)

Reached through the neon EXIT door in Level 4 (there is one most of the time).

- **The bookshop:** a huge, bright, almost silent bookshop that looks like the way out. Drop ceiling full of fluorescent panels, dark carpet, row after row of empty oak shelving, tall slatted A-frame displays, sage-green pillars covered in yellow THE END posters and sticky notes, curved service desks with old beige computers, and a yellow bulkhead at the back with metal letters: **THE END**. The glass front doors show only white.
- **Sound:** a slow piano somewhere, the whole time, and the buzz of the computers. Nothing else. There's no boss music and no boss health bar: the level just doesn't let you go.
- **Your flashlight doesn't work here.** Only the computers do.
- **You can't die here.** If the Partygoers get you, your mind goes or the host catches you, you wake up at the front desk (or at the start of the hallway) and carry on.
- **How it goes:**
  1. The front desk computer tells you which desk to go to. One computer is awake, its cursor blinking. Stand at it and it types for you, slowly. Partygoers come out of the shelves while it does.
  2. When it finishes, the screen fills with white: a **field** of long grass under a white sky, low hills, and a vending machine standing on its own. Reach the machine and use it (it gives you Almond Water), and the library comes back for you.
  3. Each time you come back, the shop is worse: lights out, **walls appearing** behind you where there weren't any, then **people you can't quite perceive** standing in the aisles (they fade as you get close), and something very tall in a black suit watching you from the end of an aisle, gone when you approach. The bulkhead reads **THE END IS NEAR**. The field gets worse too: the machine moves when you look away, shelves and ceiling tiles stand in the grass, and yellow figures creep closer whenever you're not looking.
  4. After the third time: **THE END IS HERE.** Every screen says it. The **host** walks after you, steadily, everywhere, and a door at the back of the shop opens onto a hallway that loops forever. Halfway along, a passage leads off each side; follow the **piano** (it comes from the right way). Three right turns and there's a neon EXIT at the end of the hallway.
- **The way out:** sunlight, real sunlight, and a house on a hill with its porch light on and its door open. The piano finishes. **Welcome home** to **Level 94**.

## Level 94 — Motion (Survival Difficulty 3)

Reached by escaping Level 601.

- **Looks handmade:** everything has a stop-motion, crafted look, with ink outlines, flat painted colour and grainy film that only moves twelve frames a second. The Animations and the King move at twelve frames a second too.
- **The town:** a 1930s town of little painted clapboard houses with gable roofs, picket fences and cobbled paths, dirt roads, round-fendered old cars, lamp posts and benches. You arrive inside one of the houses, furnished in 1930s–50s style (sofa, armchair, cathedral radio, rug, floor lamp, pictures). A three-tier **fountain** of Almond Water in the plaza: press **E** next to it to drink and fill a bottle. **Almond Milk vans** with their back doors open: walk up to one to take a bottle of **Almond Milk** (steadies your mind and heals a little).
- **Siren poles:** horn speakers on tall poles round the plaza play a happy, tinny cartoon tune all day, and bounce along with it.
- **Day and night:** a full day lasts seven minutes. At dusk the sky turns orange and the music winds down like a gramophone until it stops. At **night** the lamps, windows and porch lights come on, and the **Animations** come out: black rubber-hose cartoon characters with pie-cut eyes, wide grins and big white gloves. If one spots you, it does a double take (slide whistle), then chases you, fast. Get inside a lit house and they lose interest. Smilers, Skin-Stealers, Hounds, Deathmoths (circling the street lamps) and Deathrats come out at night too. At dawn the Animations fold up flat and slide away under the doors.
- **The hills:** green rounded hills, forever. Old cars and milk vans parked in the middle of nowhere, whole sets of furniture with no room around them, single wallpapered walls with their furniture, lone houses, bits of fence, and a white **water tower** on a hill near town (its tap gives Almond Water).
- **The Castle of Transparent Mountain:** a castle like a tall white grain elevator, apparently floating in the sky. Under it is a mountain of glass you can barely see: find it and walk up. Inside, the cartoon look drops away. It's a bright fun-house that's bigger inside than out:
  - an entrance hall with portraits of the King, a blinding white room, a **ball pit** (wade through it), a **playground** (slide, swings, climbing frame, seesaw, roundabout), an **animation studio** with lightbox desks, cels on a line, a rostrum camera and a **model of the town**, and a toy room (blocks, a rocking horse, a jack-in-the-box, toy chests with supplies).
  - **Robo-Men**, wind-up tin robot toys, patrol the rooms. They leave you alone until they see you touch something, then they come for you.
  - The grand hall loops forever until you've been **invited**. The invitation is on the edge of the model town, and taking it sets off every Robo-Man watching.
- **The Animated King:** on his throne at the end of a long red carpet. He talks, then sets you a test: the lights go down, everything turns black and white, and animated versions of your nightmares (a Hound, a Smiler, a Partygoer, a Deathmoth) come at you for a minute. Weapons stun them. Last the minute and he laughs, claps, and sends you off in a burst of light: back to the town, or to one of the main levels.

## Stickers, the Field Guide, and tonight's tape

Little things to come back for:

- **Stickers:** 27 small goals ("Get out of Level 0 in under a minute", "Sink the Thing on Level 7", "Fill all four backpack slots", "Make it to dawn in Level 94", "Pass through 8 levels in one run"...). Each pops up as a sticker when you earn it and goes on the sticker board in the Archive.
- **The Field Guide:** photograph an entity (V or the camera button) and it's documented: your photo becomes its picture in the guide, with its description. 23 to find, including the Host, the Animations, the Robo-Men, the Animated King, the Thing on Level 7, the children of Level 14, and Enric.
- **Tape stamps:** get out of a level alive and its tape gets a red SURVIVED stamp; each tape also counts how many times you got lost there (☠).
- **Your route:** the death and escape screens show every level the run went through (L0 → L1 → L2...), and any stickers you earned on the way. The menu remembers your longest trip.
- **Tonight's tape:** one seed a day, the same for everyone. Play it from the menu; the menu remembers how far you got.

## The archive and the camera

The main menu's **Archive** shows every level as a VHS tape on a shelf, with your newest photo of it as the cover. Levels you haven't found yet are blank tapes marked **???** until you reach them. Pick a tape to watch your footage of that level (step through it, save a photo to your device or erase it), read about the level, see how often you've been there, and play it.

Press **V** (or the camera button at the top right) to take a photo. It comes out like old camcorder footage: low resolution, smeared colour, tracking noise, a date and time burned into the corner. Photos are kept in your browser.


Every run comes from a seed, which shows on the pause, death and escape screens. Type a seed on the main menu to replay a layout.

## Entity library

Reusable entities live in `src/entities/lib/`. Level 2 uses Hounds, Smilers, Skin-Stealers, Deathmoths and Clumps, Level 3 uses Hounds and Skin-Stealers, Level 4 uses Hounds, Dullers and Smilers, and Level 5 uses Deathmoths, Hounds, Woodlins, Skin-Stealers, Watchers, Nguithr'xurh, Growlers, Deathrats, Wranglers and Samantha; Facelings, Partygoers and Wretches aren't placed in a level yet. Each has its own model, procedural skin, animations, sounds and AI (idle, movement, detection, attack), and they're lit by the level's lighting and your flashlight.

| Entity | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Smiler** | Glowing eyes and a grin, only in the dark. Drifts closer while you're in the dark with it, lunges if you linger or shine a light on it, then vanishes. |
| **Hound** | A hairless human body on all fours. Hunts by sound (sprinting carries far) and sprints in bursts. Stare it down while standing still and it hesitates. |
| **Faceling** | Ordinary people with blank faces. `passive` ones stop and watch you; `hostile` and `child` ones stalk, then chase. |
| **Skin-Stealer** | Looks like a lost wanderer, waving and calling for help. Get close, turn your back, or wait too long, and it sheds its disguise. |
| **Deathmoth** | Dog-sized moths that circle lights and get drawn to your flashlight. `drone` mostly flutters around you; `soldier` dives at you. |
| **Clump** | Fused limbs and flesh. Hides in tight spaces, lunges when you pass, then drags itself after you. |
| **Duller** | A washed-out grey figure that fades when still. Unpredictable moods; its touch festers (health drains for a while). |
| **Partygoer** | Yellow, smiling =). Dances, waves, invites you over, then the smile tears wider. |
| **Wretch** | Skinless, hunched, lopsided. Twitches and shambles until provoked (proximity or light), then charges in a zig-zag. |
| **Woodlin** | A small jointed figure of dark lacquered wood, like a forgotten mannequin. Perfectly still while watched; look away and it clatters closer, then lunges. |
| **Watcher** (Entity 96) | A giant floating eyeball trailing optic nerves. Glides silently; when it fixes on you its pupil swells and glows, then a beam turns you to grey dust. Break line of sight before it fires. |
| **Nguithr'xurh** | Grey, insect-like, fourteen jointed limbs and mandibles. Hangs upside down on the ceiling beside its round web, creeps overhead, drops on you, then climbs back up. `opts.ceiling` sets the ceiling height. |
| **Growler** | A hulk of twisted cables with fizzing copper ends. Hits incredibly hard but is dim: charges noise in a straight line, slams into walls and is stunned. |
| **Deathrat** | Rats adapted to the Backrooms. Forage in darts, flee light and attention, bite when cornered. `grey`, `pale` (eyeless, for dark levels), `rust` (industrial). |
| **Wrangler** | A massive scaled snake with a `human` or `insect` face. Burrows under floors and walls, surfaces near you and sways; watching it draws you toward it, then it strikes. |
| **Samantha** (Entity 26) | A talking black-and-white cat with green eyes and sharp teeth. Follows you and asks for meat; feed her (`player.meat`) for a psychic reading and some sanity. Provoke her (light in her eyes, running at her) or don't pay, and she attacks. Emits `{ type: 'speak', text }` events. |

To use them in a level:

```js
import { EntityManager } from '../../entities/lib/index.js';
// in the level constructor
this.entities = new EntityManager({ scene, world: this.world, audio: ctx.audio, rng: this.rng, shared });
this.entities.spawnFar('hound', player, { min: 15, max: 30 });
this.entities.spawn('faceling', { x, z, variant: 'hostile' });
// in update(dt, player)
const res = this.entities.update(dt, player, ctx);
if (res.dead) ctx.onDeath(res.cause);
// in dispose()
this.entities.dispose();
```

Open **`entities.html`** to inspect them. Pick an entity, switch poses (idle, move, detect, attack) and variants, toggle the lights and your flashlight, or turn on **Live AI** to let it come for you (the camera). Drag to orbit, and scroll or pinch to zoom.

## The backpack

Everything you pick up goes into your backpack, and it only holds **4 items**. It shows under your stats; open it to read what you're carrying, use it or drop it. When it's full, pickups stay where they are (crates stay shut) until you make room. Dropped items lie on the floor, and you can pick them up again. Your backpack comes with you between levels.

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| **Almond Water** | Drink it (E, or its slot) to restore sanity. |
| **Batteries** | Recharge the flashlight. |
| **First aid kit** | Heal 45 health. |
| **Meat** (Level 5's Homely Hotel) | Eat it, or keep it: Samantha trades readings for meat. |
| **Lead pipe** (weapon) | Swing at whatever is in front of you: it's stunned for about 4.5 seconds. Bends after 4 hits. |
| **Road flare** (weapon) | Lights the area red and stuns everything nearby that can see it for 7 seconds; anything that comes close while it burns (9 s) reels back. One use. |

Weapons only buy you time: stunned entities freeze and reel, then carry on. In Level 0 they drive the Stalker off for a while. They do nothing to *her* in Level 1.5. Pipes and flares turn up in crates and lying about in Levels 0, 2, 3, 4 and 5.

Stamina lasts longer than it used to (about 9 seconds of running) and comes back faster.

The Settings screen has mouse and touch sensitivity, FOV, volume, invert Y, graphics quality and an FPS counter. On "Auto", graphics quality steps down if the device can't keep up.

## Adding a level

1. Create `src/levels/levelN/LevelN.js` exporting a definition `{ id, number, name, title, difficulty, next, create(ctx) }`. `registry.js` documents the instance interface.
2. Grid-style levels can reuse `GridMap` and `GridWorld` with their own generator, textures and lighting.
3. Register the level in `src/levels/registry.js`.

Levels hand off with `ctx.onEscape(targetId, 'noclip' | 'door' | 'walk')`, and player stats carry over. An exit that targets a level that isn't built yet shows a "coming soon" screen, using the names in `UPCOMING`. Level 4's and Level 5's exits already target `level9` and `level71`, Level 6's target `level129` and `level6_1`, Level 7's target `level8`, `level9`, `level83` and `level880`. (Level 601's `level94` is now built.)
